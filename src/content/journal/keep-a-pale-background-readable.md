A pale page can feel spacious and still be difficult to read. Texture adds local variation behind every letter, while thin type can lose definition against the lightest parts of the surface. Treat the reading layer and the material layer as separate decisions.

## Start with the small labels

Put a price, a date and a short paragraph over the background. These are more useful tests than a large decorative title. If you need to lean closer to read a label, increase its size or darken it before adding more visual effects.

### Give long copy a calm surface

A solid or quieter panel behind a longer paragraph can make the reading area more predictable. Match that panel closely to the surrounding page so it feels like a natural pause in the texture.

## Compare two controls

First adjust the text colour while leaving the background unchanged. Then restore the original text and soften the texture. This reveals which change gives the largest improvement without flattening the whole page.

> A subtle surface can remain visible while the text stays firmly in front of it.

## Check more than one view

- A narrow screen with a long title wrapping onto several lines.
- A bright display where the texture nearly disappears.
- A normal reading distance with no browser zoom.
- A long paragraph beside a dense product image.

## Use measurement alongside judgement

Check text and background colours with a contrast tool as part of the review. A varied texture also deserves visual inspection because different areas sit behind different letters. Keep a plain-background comparison available when deciding whether the material layer helps the page.
