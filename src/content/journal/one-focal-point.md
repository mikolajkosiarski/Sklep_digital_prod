When every element asks for attention, a page can feel crowded even with few objects on it. Decide what people should notice first. That choice gives the title, ornament and supporting information a useful relationship.

## Name the main subject

Write down the first thing the composition should communicate: a product, a title or a particular shape. Give that element the clearest position. Place the supporting information only after the subject feels settled.

### Compare silhouette and density

An ornament does not need to be the largest object to dominate a layout. A dense dark centre may attract attention before a larger area of thin lettering. Compare the overall dark shapes as well as the dimensions of each element.

## Remove one thing at a time

Hide a small decoration, then a secondary line of copy, then an extra border. Compare each version with the original. Keep the elements that clarify the main subject or contribute something distinct to the atmosphere.

> Empty space gives the eye time to recognise the shape beside it.

## Make the hierarchy visible

- Give the main subject the strongest placement.
- Use a smaller, quieter style for supporting information.
- Leave a clear gap between unrelated elements.
- Keep decorative detail away from the smallest type.

## Review the thumbnail

Reduce the composition to a small preview and look at it briefly. If the main subject disappears into several equally strong shapes, adjust their spacing or scale. Return to the full view to check the details once the hierarchy is clear.
