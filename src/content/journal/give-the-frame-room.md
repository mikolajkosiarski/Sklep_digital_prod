A detailed border changes the way an image sits on a page. Its outer corners may feel generous while the opening remains quite narrow. Start with that opening and give the image enough space to remain recognisable inside it.

## Work from the centre out

Place the main subject first, then bring the frame around it on a separate layer. Keep the subject away from the inward-facing branches. When a face, a line of text or a small symbol touches a flourish, the two shapes can become difficult to separate.

### Keep a visible gap

Try a slightly smaller image before enlarging the border. A thin area of clear background can separate two complex silhouettes without adding another stroke or panel. Compare the result at the size people will actually see.

## Preserve the original proportions

Resize the frame from a corner with its proportions locked. If the opening still feels wrong, choose another frame or change the crop of the image. Stretching one axis changes the thickness and rhythm of every ornamental detail.

> Fit the content to the opening. Let the frame keep its own proportions.

## Make three versions

- A full frame around a smaller image.
- A larger crop with a clear margin at the corners.
- Two decorative edges with more open space between them.

Keep the same subject and background in all three. Changing one part at a time makes the differences easier to see.

## Check the smallest version

Look for lost detail, accidental tangencies and corners that feel heavier than the centre. If the thumbnail needs more room, simplify the framing before making the entire card larger. A strong silhouette usually survives the smaller size more comfortably.
