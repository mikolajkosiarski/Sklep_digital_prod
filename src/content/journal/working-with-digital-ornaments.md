An intricate ornament can carry a composition by itself. Start with one design and decide what it should do: frame a title, draw attention to an image, or become the main subject. Give it a clear role before adding more.

## Choose the focal point

Place the main subject first. If the lettering needs to lead, keep the strongest ornament away from the words. If the artwork is the subject, make the supporting text smaller and leave a generous gap around it. Two equally dense elements placed close together can make the page difficult to read.

## Fit the opening, not just the outline

A decorative frame has an outer silhouette and an inner space. Fit a photograph or title to that inner opening. Check the corners and any flourishes that reach inward; an image that fits the outer rectangle can still cover important details.

Keep the original proportions while resizing. If the shape does not suit the content, try a different frame before stretching it.

### Leave a quiet edge

Keep a small clear area between the most detailed part of the ornament and the nearest line of type. Adjust that gap at the final viewing size rather than relying on the zoomed-in canvas.

> The empty area around an ornament is part of its shape in the finished layout.

## Make three simple comparisons

- Compare one large ornament with two smaller ones.
- Compare a centred arrangement with artwork placed toward one edge.
- Compare a full border with decoration on only two sides.

Keep the content unchanged during these tests so you can judge the arrangement itself.

## Check the small version

View the composition at its final display size. Fine branches may disappear, neighbouring shapes may merge, and a comfortable gap on a large canvas may become cramped. Simplify where necessary, then check again without zooming in.
